# 0031 The one door with no number

**Status:** implemented
**Date:** 2026-10-20
**Built:** 2026-10-20, the same day it was written.

## Goal

Cantor's diagonal, walked rather than explained, as the exception to the sign
hanging over the hotel's corridor.

Spec 0029 put that sign up and it says THE ROOMS DO NOT RUN OUT. Spec 0030
made that true in front of you: the hotel is full, somebody arrives, one move
houses them, and it is full again with nobody gone. Every arrival so far has
been housed.

This is the one that cannot be.

## Why it is the sequel and not a new direction

The hotel is ℵ₀: countable infinity, and its whole trick is that a full hotel
always has room. The diagonal is the other half of that story, and it is the
more startling half, because it says some infinities cannot be listed at all.

It belongs here rather than anywhere because **it is a proof you can walk**,
and walking is what this building is for. Spec 0018 made a distance walkable,
thirty three kilometres of solar system. Spec 0030 made a count walkable.
This makes an argument walkable, which is a thing none of the rooms have
tried.

And it pairs with the room next door rather than standing beside it. The
hotel's sign is thirty paces away and this is its exception. Neither is as
good alone.

## Behavior

### The door with no number

**In the corridor, among the numbered ones, a short walk in.**

Every door in that corridor has a plaque with a number on it, which spec 0029
built and spec 0030 made follow you. This one has a plaque and nothing on it.

That is the whole of the signposting and it is enough, because it is the one
thing in forty doors that is different, and because it is also the joke: the
room that proves not everything can be numbered is behind the one door that
is not.

Near enough the lobby that you pass it. The building's own record on things
nobody finds is bad and spec 0020 says so in as many words.

### The register

An endless list of endless entries, which is what a hotel with a room for
every number would have to keep.

Rows stacked, each row a string of characters, and **the wall does not move.**
Row n is in the same place every time you look at it, which is what a book
written on a wall is.

~~The rows recede the way the corridor does and for the same reason: drawn
around wherever you are standing rather than a fixed count.~~

Struck 2026-10-21. That is the corridor's answer and it is wrong here. The
corridor's doors do not follow you either: room 40 is always in the same place
and only the window of *drawn* ones moves. Sliding the register's contents
along to keep the diagonal in front of the reader made the wall never the same
twice, and Jake walked it and said it seemed to move with him. It did.

**So the book has a last row, and this spec no longer claims otherwise.** The
diagonal can be no longer along a wall than the wall is high, because the cell
taken from row n is n down and n along: the walk is the height. The room is
four and a bit metres now, nearly twice the corridor it is off, which a hall
of records would be anyway, and the walk is the twenty rows that fit. What is
shown is the first twenty entries of something endless, said plainly, the same
way spec 0029 says eleven million is not infinity.

**One texture an alphabet, not one a row.** The corridor's plaques are a
texture each because every one says a different number. A register is the
same handful of characters over and over, so it is one small texture per
character and an instance per place, which is what this engine is for.

### The walk

You walk along the register and the diagonal climbs away from you: the first
character of the first row, the second of the second, the third of the third.

At each step the character you pass is taken, changed, and added to what you
are carrying. That is the whole of the proof and it is five words long: change
the diagonal, and what you have differs from every row in at least one place.

**It has to be yours rather than watched.** A camera that flies the diagonal
while you hold still is a cutscene, and this building has never had one. What
you are holding grows as you walk and stops when you stop.

**And the wall has to show that.** Noted 2026-10-21. The whole diagonal was
lit from the moment you came through the door, so walking the room changed
nothing on screen: Jake walked it and said he could see no actions in the
register room. A cell behind the walk is lit now and a cell ahead of it is
only marked out, so the staircase fills in as you go.

**The room also had no fittings and no light of its own**, which left the
head of the page an unlit corner at the far end of a nine metre hall with
nothing pointing at it. There is one sconce in here and it is over room 1,
which makes the start of the walk the lit end of a dark room. That is a thing
people go and look at without being told to.

### And then you take it to the desk

This is the end of it and the reason the two rooms are thirty paces apart.

You carry what you made back to the hotel's desk. The desk has housed every
arrival since the building opened, by shuffling, by doubling, and by the
prime powers if spec 0030 ever gains them.

~~It cannot house this one. That is the first time that desk says no, and it
says it about a guest you wrote yourself, one line at a time, out of the
register that was supposed to have everybody in it.~~

Struck 2026-10-21 and built out the same day. Jake walked up to the desk, read
the refusal, and said that if he is holding a number and there is no limit to
how many rooms there are then he should be able to get a room.

He is right, and the refusal was false. A number is finite, the rooms are the
counting numbers, and the move spec 0030 already built houses it. Cantor's
argument never said one guest cannot be housed. It says no list can be
finished, and the way to show that is to **house them and let whoever walked
the book walk it again**. Spec 0032 has all of it, including the two faults
underneath: the entries ended at column 24, and the diagonal was handed to you
in the doorway before you had taken a step.

## What this must not become

**A puzzle with an answer.** Nothing here is typed, chosen or guessed. The
diagonal is not a riddle, it is a construction, and the player's part is to
walk it and watch the thing come into their hands.

**A cutscene.** See above. If it plays while you stand there it is a video
with a room round it.

**An explanation.** There is no panel of text stating Cantor's theorem. The
hotel does not explain itself either: its sign is six words and the rest is
what you watch happen.

## What building it found

**There was exactly one place a door could go.** Rooms stand 0.75 apart along
the corridor, alternating sides, and a door is 1.05 wide, so same-side doors
are 1.5 apart with 0.45 between them. Nothing fits anywhere. What fits is the
slot the rooms were never using: everything moved one place along, and the
blank door dropped into the alternating pattern exactly, on the even side and
1.5 from room 2, which is the spacing every other same-side pair has.

So it stands **where room nought would be**, which is better than the spec
asked for. The door that is not a room is in the place that is not a counting
number.

Moving the rooms broke `aleph::nearest`, which is the inverse of where a room
stands, and the corridor was numbered one way and drawn another until its own
test said so.

**The book was a staircase first.** Each row was stepped along the wall *and*
up it, so what came out was a diagonal band rather than a page with a diagonal
through it. The wall is a fixed grid now and the register scrolls across it as
you walk, which is also how it has no last row and no last column.

Then the page was anchored to the room's east end rather than to the walker,
so the wall in front of you was blank once you had walked past the door.

**A blank plaque is what a broken plaque looks like.** The door was given a
plaque with nothing on it, which is the whole of the signposting this spec
asked for. Jake walked the corridor and reported it as a fault, and he was
right to: that morning, room 104's plaque had come out blank because its
texture was never made, so the same pixels meant both "this is the register"
and "the number failed to draw". It has no plaque at all now and is painted
rather than veneered, which is what a door that is not a room looks like.

**And the wall between the two rooms was built twice.** The blank door is on
the even side, so this room's near wall stood in exactly the plane of the
corridor's, which is two faces in one plane and speckles as you move: the
fault this building has fought seven times, and the eighth was here. The
corridor's wall is cut for the doorway now, the way the gallery's walls are
cut for the hotel and the back room, and this room builds none of that side.

**And the wall never said what it was.** Jake walked the room and asked where
Cantor's diagonalization fits, which is the honest verdict on a register that
is an anonymous grid of digits: it could be a timetable, and nothing on it
said it belonged to the hotel outside.

So **the rows are rooms**. Row n carries room n's number at the start of it,
borrowing that room's plaque rather than keeping a second account of what the
number looks like. With that one label the rest reads without explaining: the
lit diagonal is one digit taken from each room in turn, what you carry differs
from room n's entry at place n, and the desk refusing it stops being a rule
the building imposes and becomes the only thing it could say.

Putting the numbers on also found that the rows were written backwards. The
columns ran along `-x`, and facing that wall you look down `-z`, so your right
hand points along `+x`: every entry read right to left and the room number
landed at the end of the line.

**And the sight went straight through both rooms' walls.** Spec 0003's sight
takes the nearest thing the ray meets among the things you can use, and stops
at `Room::opaque`, which was the hall's walls and nothing else. These two
rooms were built after it and were never added, so standing in the register
you could point at the wall and the building would name a corridor door
behind it: "Room 8 has somebody in it".

The book also answered for the whole far wall rather than the part it is
written on, so pointing at a bare stretch still said "The register".

Its two end walls then had the same fault one plane over, by stopping flush
with that wall's face. They stop half a thickness short, which leaves a slot
too narrow to stand in, closed on every side, and no two faces near each
other.

## Acceptance criteria

- The unnumbered door is in the drawn window wherever you stand near the
  lobby, so it is passed rather than hunted. — `aleph::tests::the_blank_door_is_passed`
- Its plaque is blank and every other plaque in view is
  not. — `tests::one_door_in_the_corridor_has_no_number`
- The book answers for any row and any column, however far past what is
  drawn. — `register::tests::there_is_always_another_row`
- And the strip over the corridor's wall is closed, since this room is nearly
  twice its height and borrows it. — `register::tests::the_strip_over_the_borrowed_wall_is_closed`
- The diagonal takes the nth character of the nth row, wherever the walk
  starts. — `register::tests::the_diagonal_climbs_as_it_recedes`
- What you carry differs from every row that has been walked past, in at
  least one place, which is the proof
  itself. — `register::tests::what_you_hold_is_on_no_row`
- And it grows only while you move, so it is walked rather than
  watched. — `register::tests::standing_still_writes_nothing`
- And the wall shows it: the diagonal is lit behind the walk and marked out
  ahead of it. — `register::tests::the_diagonal_fills_in_as_you_walk`
- The room's one lamp is in a fitting, and the fitting is over the head of the
  page rather than the middle of the
  floor. — `register::tests::every_lamp_is_in_a_fitting`
- ~~The desk refuses this one and has refused no other.~~ Struck with the
  section above. It houses them, and spec 0032 holds what it costs
  it. — `tests::the_desk_houses_the_diagonal`
- The sight does not reach through either room's walls, which it did until
  both were added to the list it
  stops at. — `register::tests::the_sight_does_not_reach_through_these_walls`
- Every row carries the room number whose entry it is, which is what connects
  the wall to the hotel. — `tests::every_row_of_the_register_is_a_room`
- This room and the corridor it is off do not both build the wall between
  them, and neither does anything of this room's end in the plane of it,
  which is spec 0014's fault stated for a new
  pair. — `register::tests::the_wall_between_is_built_once`

### Verified by hand

- The blank plaque is noticed without anybody being told about it.
- Walking the diagonal feels like doing something rather than triggering
  something.
- The desk saying no lands, having said yes every time before.
