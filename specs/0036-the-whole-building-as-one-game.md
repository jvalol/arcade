# 0036 The whole building as one game

**Status:** draft
**Date:** 2026-10-22, amended 2026-11-07

## Goal

Jake, 2026-10-20, as a thought at the end of a message: could the whole
experience be a game, the hall and the garden and the solar system and all of
it, Dan Brown style. Then, when asked what it would take: a game like that
needs a worthy goal, and sufficient clues.

This arcade is eleven rooms that are each good and that nothing joins. You
walk from one to the next and the building never asks you for anything. A
trail through all of them is the first idea anybody has had that uses the
whole building rather than adding to it.

## What this settles, and the building settled it

**The player is a person in the fiction.** That was recorded as the question
to answer first, because it decides whether this is a trail or a story, and
it is the same question that blocked the hotel in spec 0029.

It is already answered, in copy that shipped without anybody noticing they
had answered it:

- The desk counts `housed since you came in`. Not since the building opened:
  since *you* arrived. It has been keeping a number about the player for two
  days.
- The register says `each line gives up its figure as you pass`, and the desk
  says `Room 1, then, and everybody moves up` to somebody standing in front
  of it.
- And spec 0032's whole ending is the desk taking something out of the
  player's hand and writing it in a book.

So the player is a guest: somebody the building speaks to, counts, and
accepts things from. **And never somebody you see.** Eleven rooms and nobody
has drawn them, and spec 0033 drew everyone else instead.

That is the settlement, and it costs nothing to keep: a trail does not need
an avatar. The hall of mirrors does, and that is that room's problem, not
this one's.

## The rule that keeps it from being a scavenger hunt

**A room's clue is the answer to the thing that room already asks.**

Every room here is one idea with one thing to do in it. Walk the register's
diagonal and you come out holding something. Check somebody in and watch a
corridor empty. Walk the long hall until the counts come apart. Ride to the
sun at true scale.

The clue is what that already gives you. Nothing is hidden in a corner,
nothing glints, nothing exists only to be found. If a room needs an object
planted in it to join the trail, the trail is wrong about that room.

This is the whole design and everything else is detail. It is also what
decides whether the trail is worth walking: done this way, following it *is*
doing the building properly, and the clue is the receipt.

## What it leads to

**A room that does not exist yet.** The building already hides spaces that do
not obey the rest of it: behind the near wall per spec 0011, behind a plinth
per spec 0021, and spec 0020's own goal calls the hidden wing "the part
nobody finds". Its record on things nobody finds is bad and it says so.

Three rooms are waiting without reasons to be built: an imaginarium with an
Escher staircase, a carnival hall of mirrors, and a theatre you sit in.
**Which of those is at the end is Jake's, and it is the one decision this
spec does not make.** The trail would give whichever it is the thing none of
them has, which is a reason to exist.

Inventing a fourth room as the prize is how you get a room nobody wanted.

## What the building has not got

**State across rooms.** The hotel, its corridor, the register and the long
hall now refer to each other: the desk's move changes the register, the
register's diagonal comes back to the desk, and the long hall only means
anything because the corridor beside it exists. That is four rooms.

The other seven have none of it. The hall, the nook, the cellar, the baths,
the garden, the space behind the wall and the back room are each sealed, and
nothing anywhere knows what you have already done. That is the real work in
this spec and it is not a trail, it is plumbing.

## What this must not become

**A checklist.** No panel of ticks, no "3 of 8 found". The building has never
had a HUD beyond one line under the sight and should not start.

**Signposting the hidden rooms.** Spec 0020's worn lane in the carpet is the
model: a thing that happened rather than a thing somebody put up. A clue that
says GO LEFT is a sign.

**A reason to make the rooms worse.** Every room here works standing alone
and must keep working standing alone. Somebody who never finds the trail
should not be able to tell it is there.

**And that rule is about the building, not about everything that mentions
it.** It was written as though it covered the site too, and since
2026-11-07 it does not: the clip on the front page ends on Bifröst, so the
first thing anybody learns about this building is that it has a prize in
it. Jake's call, with the take in front of him. The argument for it is that
a trailer whose job is to make somebody want to walk the place cannot also
be the thing that hides what is worth walking to, and the trail is still
not findable from inside, which is where the rule bites: the lane in the
carpet is still a thing that happened and nothing in the building says go
left. What is spoiled is that there is an end. Where it is and what it
asks are untouched.

**A story.** The player is a guest, not a protagonist. Nobody is rescued and
nothing is explained at the end.

## What was waiting on it, and is not now

**What is at the end.** Not one of the three rooms, which is the answer this
spec expected and argued itself out of in the same breath. Jake, 2026-11-04:
the end should be a reward, a prize, something like a rainbow bridge to
Elysium or Valhalla. That is Bifröst and spec 0054 builds it: finish what
every room asks and a bridge comes up out of the field, west of the garden,
and you can walk it.

It answers this spec's own test for whether a thing is worth adding, which
is that it use the whole building rather than add to it. The prize is height
and the only sight of the arcade from outside itself. Eleven rooms and you
have been in all of them; nobody has ever seen the building whole.

**The plumbing.** Spec 0051 built it for the two rooms that asked anything at
the time. All eight of `trail::Asked` are wired now, in the rooms that
already ask them, and the bridge reads the list rather than a count: a room
that starts asking something joins the condition without either spec being
touched.

## What is still open

**Nothing, as of 2026-11-06.** Valhalla is
[0056](0056-valhalla.md) and the gate and its riddle are
[0058](0058-a-gate-and-a-riddle.md), both implemented. The riddle meets the
test this section sets below, which is the part worth keeping: the watchman
has never seen the arcade, so what he asks is what it looks like from the
far end of the bridge, and the answer is the lantern spec 0057 put on the
imaginarium's roof. There is nothing to look up and nobody who has not
climbed the bridge is ever asked.

This heading said otherwise until 2026-11-10 and sent a reader looking for
work that was four days finished. Spec 0055's own "this does not build"
section and spec 0037's tail were stale the same day, which is three in one
building. What follows is left as written, because it is why those two specs
exist.

**The room behind Heimdall, and the puzzle he asks you first.** Spec 0055
built the threshold and the watchman and said there was nothing behind him.
Jake, 2026-11-04, having walked it: Heimdall should let you into a room, and
there should be a final puzzle with him, and the room should be Van Gogh or
Kinkade inspired.

Heimdall is the last gate and what is behind him is Valhalla, which is
where something stunning should be. The thing he asks is a riddle rather
than a game, sphinx-like.

So the fourth room this spec warns about is being built after all, and the
warning stands as the thing to be careful of rather than as a reason to
stop. What makes it not a room nobody wanted is the same test as everything
else here: it has to use the whole building rather than add to it. For the
riddle that means an answer you can only have from having walked the
building. Spec 0055 carries the rest.

## 2026-11-10: three of the rooms it counts are one room now

**Spec 0062 took the hotel's corridor and the long hall out of the
building.** What is left of that wing is the lobby, the desk and the
register behind one door, which this spec counted as three of its eleven
rooms and two of the four it said had state across them.

The trail is unchanged. Both of its stations in that wing survive, because
both were in the half that was something you do: walking the register's
diagonal, and handing what it leaves you to the desk. The eight stations are
still eight.

Two sentences above are now false and are left standing because the argument
round them is not. "Check somebody in and watch a corridor empty" and "walk
the long hall until the counts come apart" were this spec's own examples of
a room whose clue is the answer to the thing it already asks. The first was
a room whose one thing to do was watching, which is why spec 0062 exists.
The second was good and is the real loss, written up there.
